Same cells.
Different decisions.

Genes make many RNA isoforms, and single-cell data usually counts them as one. We build the technology and software to count isoforms in every cell, and use them to find cell states in cancer and autoimmunity that gene counts cannot separate.

Why we exist

Most single-cell studies count genes. That reduces about twenty thousand protein-coding genes to one number each and hides the more than two hundred thousand isoforms they produce. The differences lost in that count, such as which exon a transcript includes or which 3′ end it uses, can change what a cell does, including in drug-resistant cancer and in autoimmune disease. We build the technology and software to measure those differences in individual cells.

Research pillars

Measure · Quantify · Integrate · Apply

A benchtop single-cell platform, a long-read isoform pipeline, methods that connect its data to existing atlases, and two biology programs, one in cancer and one in autoimmunity.

Measure
BenchDrop-seq platform

Single-cell long-read RNA-seq without a microfluidics instrument: PIP-seq capture plus Oxford Nanopore sequencing on a standard lab bench. PIP-ATAC applies it to chromatin.
Quantify
Bagpiper isoform pipeline

Turns Nanopore reads into isoform-by-cell count matrices: spacer-anchored barcode recovery, assignment by splice junction, and EM resolution of reads that fit several isoforms.
Integrate
Cross-platform projection

A two-layer annotation that transfers cell identity from short-read atlases onto BenchDrop-seq data, and methods that align RNA, chromatin and isoform measurements in each cell.
Apply
Cancer & autoimmunity

Drug-tolerant persister cells in NRAS-mutant melanoma, and how splicing changes immune cell fate in multiple sclerosis and EBV infection.
